## Title
The Stone Path of Harrow’s End

## Premise
Ilse, a pragmatic farmer inheriting a dying pilgrimage road, must choose between preserving the ancestral duty of her uncle or protecting her family from an unseen, encroaching threat that follows the last travelers north, forcing her to confront whether memory is a burden or a bridge.

## Themes
Duty versus survival, the weight of history, the morality of isolation, the persistence of memory in silence.

## Voice
Third-person limited, past tense, measured and austere, rendering landscape as moral geography with aphoristic precision.

## Characters
**Ilse Vane**: protagonist — wants to keep her farm profitable and safe — voice: practical, terse, observant — eyes: grey — hair: brown — age: 26
  Active Arc: In Chapter 7, Ilse refuses the Baron’s foreman’s offer of payment to remove the waystones, choosing integrity over immediate security.

**Uncle Eamon**: deceased — left the road as a burden — voice: recorded in ledger as stoic, silent — eyes: unknown — hair: white — age: 58 (at death)
  Active Arc: His ledger entries in Chapter 4 reveal he did not just maintain the road; he actively turned away those who sought to stop the travelers’ progress.

**Baron Kaelen’s Foreman, Torvin**: antagonist — wants the stones for his lord’s new hall — voice: bureaucratic, dismissive, sharp — eyes: blue — hair: black — age: 40
  Active Arc: In Chapter 6, Torvin personally dismantles a waystone at dawn, testing Ilse’s resolve without waiting for her permission.

**The Traveler, Jaren**: catalyst — seeks passage north despite the danger — voice: weary, precise, lacking fear — eyes: dark — hair: black — age: 30
  Active Arc: In Chapter 9, Jaren reveals the nature of the "thing" following them, driving Ilse to understand the true cost of the road.

**Young Elara**: innocent — wants to see the sea — voice: curious, fragile, honest — eyes: green — hair: blonde — age: 8
  Active Arc: In Chapter 8, Elara’s illness forces Ilse to make a medical decision that violates the road’s quarantine rules.

## Settings
**Harrow’s End Farm**: a smallholding of stubborn earth and wind-bent wheat, smelling of damp soil and woodsmoke.
**The Pilgrim’s Road**: a cracked stone path winding through hollows, marked by standing stones that hum with cold air.
**Uncle Eamon’s Cottage**: a low stone hut near the road’s start, filled with dust and the smell of dried herbs and old paper.
**The Baron’s Keep**: a distant silhouette on the hill, cold and imposing, smelling of iron and burnt coal.
**The Northern Shore**: a desolate coastline visible only in legend, where the road ends in salt and silence.

## Plot Points
1. (setup, ch01) Ilse receives the deed to the road, assuming it is worthless land.
2. (foreshadow, ch03) Ilse finds Uncle Eamon’s ledger, noting dates of travelers who never returned.
3. (inciting incident, ch04) A bruised traveler asks the way, breaking the seven-year silence.
4. (rising action, ch06) Torvin begins removing waystones; Ilse confronts him, realizing the road’s protection is failing.
5. (midpoint, ch08) Elara falls ill; Ilse must choose between the road’s sanctity and her daughter’s life.
6. (reversal, ch10) Jaren reveals the "thing" is not a monster, but a consequence of the road’s neglect.
7. (climax, ch13) Ilse stands at the final waystone, blocking the Baron’s men and the advancing shadow.
8. (payoff, ch15) The road holds, but Ilse loses her home to the Baron’s claim.
9. (epilogue, ch18) The road continues, empty but intact, carrying the memory of those who walked it.

## Chapters
1. Ilse reads the deed, dismissing the road as dead stone and forgotten duty.
2. She walks the upper path, feeling the cold absence of footsteps where there should be warmth.
3. In the cottage, she finds the ledger, noticing the ink fades as the years pass.
4. At dusk, Jaren arrives, his staff bruised, asking for passage north when none has come in years.
5. Ilse shelters Jaren, hearing his tale of a silence that follows travelers, eating sound and light.
6. Torvin arrives at dawn, ordering the removal of the western waystone for the Baron’s foundation.
7. Ilse physically blocks Torvin, risking her livelihood to preserve the last marker of her uncle’s care.
8. Elara wakes feverish; Ilse breaks the quarantine rule, bringing a healer onto the sacred path.
9. Jaren confesses the sickness is linked to the road’s decay, and the "thing" is drawn to broken stones.
10. Ilse realizes her uncle did not guard the road from invaders, but from the entropy of forgetting.
11. The Baron sends soldiers; Ilse gathers the villagers, revealing the road’s true value as a barrier against the dark.
12. The travelers return, weakened, urging Ilse to seal the road or let the shadow pass.
13. Ilse stands alone at the final waystone, refusing to move, anchoring the road with her own will.
14. The shadow recedes, repelled by the unbroken line of stone and the memory of those who walked it.
15. The Baron’s men retreat, but seize the farm as collateral for Ilse’s defiance.
16. Ilse packs her few belongings, leaving the cottage to the dust and the ledger.
17. She walks south, watching the road behind her, now empty but intact under the moon.
18. Years later, a new traveler touches a waystone, feeling the faint warmth of Ilse’s hand.

## Pacing
Tension builds slowly through the decay of the road and the intrusion of the Baron’s men. The midpoint occurs in Chapter 8, where Ilse’s personal stakes (Elara’s health) collide with the abstract duty of the road. The climax lands in Chapter 13, where Ilse makes the irreversible choice to stand against both human authority and supernatural threat. The resolution is quiet, focusing on loss rather than victory.

## Irrevocable Loss
Ilse loses her home, Harrow’s End, to the Baron’s legal claim. She saves the road, but at the cost of her family’s stability and her place in the community. She becomes a wanderer, carrying only the memory of the stones she protected. The farm is gone, the village turns away, and she has no roots left, only the path. This loss is permanent; she never returns to live there again.
