## Title
**The Stitched Key**

## Premise
When a Cooper Union machinist is found drowned with a factory key sewn into his hem, independent investigator Pearl Casey must decode the industrial sabotage threatening New York’s new elevated railways while evading a killer who knows her every move, forcing her to choose between exposing the truth and protecting the only family she has left.

## Themes
Class warfare, the commodification of labor, the invisibility of women in law enforcement

## Voice
Close third, past tense, lean and tactile with a dry, period-appropriate wit that favors concrete observation over emotional exposition.

## Characters
**Pearl Casey**: protagonist — wants to prove her worth beyond her father’s shadow — voice: sharp, clipped, uses humor as armor — eyes: green — hair: auburn — age: 29
  Active Arc: In Chapter 8, Pearl deliberately lets herself be followed into the subway tunnels to trap the saboteur, driving the action by sacrificing her safety rather than waiting for backup.

**Sergeant Michael Doyle**: ally/NYPD — wants to keep his precinct clean but is bound by bureaucracy — voice: weary, gravelly, uses Irish lilt sparingly — eyes: grey — hair: black — age: 45
  Active Arc: In Chapter 11, Doyle bypasses orders to protect Pearl from a corrupt captain, actively choosing loyalty over career survival.

**Silas Vane**: antagonist/Industrialist — wants to monopolize transit contracts — voice: smooth, aristocratic, never raises volume — eyes: blue — hair: platinum — age: 52
  Active Arc: In Chapter 6, Vane invites Pearl to tea, not to interrogate her, but to offer her a bribe, attempting to co-opt her investigation through wealth rather than violence.

**Lao Wei**: Chinatown apothecary — wants to protect his community from gang violence — voice: cryptic, polite, speaks in riddles — eyes: brown — hair: black — age: 60
  Active Arc: In Chapter 9, Wei refuses to give Pearl the antidote for the poison found on the victim until she agrees to burn the evidence linking his son to the crime, driving the moral conflict.

**Elena Rossi**: immigrant seamstress — wants to feed her children — voice: rapid, anxious, Italian cadence — eyes: hazel — hair: black — age: 34
  Active Arc: In Chapter 4, Elena confronts Pearl about searching her home, physically blocking the doorway until Pearl proves she isn’t there for arrest, shifting the power dynamic.

## Settings
**The East River Dockyards**: smell of rotting fish and coal smoke, sound of lapping black water against rotting pilings.
**Vane’s Fifth Avenue Townhouse**: smell of beeswax and expensive tobacco, sound of a grand piano practicing scales in the next room.
**The Bowery Tenement**: smell of boiled cabbage and unwashed bodies, sound of coughing echoing through thin walls.
**Chinatown Apothecary**: smell of dried ginseng and opium, sound of rain drumming on tin roof.

## Plot Points
1. (setup, ch02) Pearl finds the key sewn into the victim’s hem — establishes the mystery.
2. (foreshadow, ch04) Elena warns Pearl about "men in grey coats" watching the tenements.
3. (midpoint, ch07) Pearl realizes the key belongs to Vane’s private office, not the factory.
4. (reversal, ch09) Wei reveals the victim was poisoned, not just drowned, changing the motive.
5. (climax, ch13) Pearl confronts Vane on the railway rooftop; he tries to push her off.
6. (payoff, ch15) Pearl burns the ledger, saving the workers but losing the legal proof.

## Chapters
1. Pearl identifies the body and notes the unnatural stitching, rejecting the official drowning ruling.
2. She visits the tenement, meeting Elena, who claims the victim was arguing about wages.
3. Sergeant Doyle dismisses the case as a robbery gone wrong, forcing Pearl to work alone.
4. Pearl returns to the tenement, finding a warning scratched into the floorboards near her boots.
5. She tracks the key’s origin to a defunct machine shop, finding fresh oil on the locks.
6. Vane invites Pearl to tea, offering money to drop the case, revealing his knowledge of her past.
7. Pearl breaks into Vane’s townhouse at night, discovering blueprints for the railway with sabotage markers.
8. She follows a suspect into the subway tunnels, realizing she is being led into a trap.
9. At the apothecary, Wei confirms the poison and demands Pearl hide his son’s involvement.
10. Pearl is attacked in an alley; Doyle saves her, but she hides the truth about the attack from him.
11. Doyle discovers Pearl’s unauthorized investigation and covers for her against his superior.
12. Pearl decodes the sabotage markers, realizing the next target is the Grand Central line.
13. She races to the construction site, confronting Vane on the unfinished steel girder.
14. Vane falls during the struggle, killed by his own greed, leaving Pearl with the evidence.
15. Pearl burns the incriminating ledger to protect the workers’ union, inheriting her father’s case books.

## Pacing
Tension builds steadily through the first five chapters via environmental threats and social barriers. The midpoint (Chapter 7) reverses the direction from criminal robbery to high-level corporate conspiracy. Peaks occur in Chapter 8 (subway trap) and Chapter 13 (rooftop fall). The climax is immediate and violent, followed by a quiet, melancholic resolution in Chapter 15 where the physical danger ends but the moral ambiguity remains.

## Irrevirable Loss
Pearl must burn the ledger that would legally convict Vane and secure justice for the victim’s family. By choosing to protect the innocent workers implicated in the sabotage, she sacrifices the official closure and the financial reward, ensuring the killer dies by accident rather than law, and leaving her with only the hollow victory of knowing the truth.
