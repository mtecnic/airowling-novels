# Editor's Letter — **The Deep Remembered**

Dear author,

Four editors read your manuscript. Each pass surfaces something different. Their voices follow.

## Where the book stands

### Story (developmental editor)

You have built a mausoleum, not a novel. The prose is suffocatingly heavy, mistaking weight for gravity. You quote Melville and Conrad, but you only mimic their vocabulary, not their moral complexity. The result is a manuscript that feels like a single mood extended across fifteen chapters: everything is tragic, everything is inevitable, nothing surprises us because we are told how to feel before we are asked to watch.

The central failure is your handling of Nemo. He is not a character; he is a force of nature that nothing touches. The premise says he requires 'two broken souls and the destruction of his own peace,' but the text denies both. Ayaanle’s mutiny in Chapter 7 is described as a choice, yet it carries no consequence other than a nod. Broussard’s betrayal in Chapter 9 is a whisper against a gale. Nemo never has to choose because he never has to pay. He is the author’s avatar, immune to the chaos he unleashes. If he is to be the villain of his own story, let the text actually wound him.

Finally, the closing is drowning in repetition. You have written the same scene—Ayaanle escaping, Thorne falling—four times in the last two chapters. You use the phrase 'the first time the destination had changed' six times. You treat the reader as if they have amnesia. Strip the redundancy. Let the ending be quiet, not loud.

### Prose (line editor)

To the Author,

You are suffocating your characters with the word 'remember.' I have found 'remember' or 'remembers' deployed as a verb in nearly every chapter, often as the pivot point for emotional revelation. In ch01, the ship 'remembered its duty'; in ch04, the ship 'remembered what he had forgotten'; in ch12, the hull 'remembered the bilge water.' This is a verbal crutch that robs your imagery of specificity. When you tell me the ship remembers, I don't see the ship; I see your authorial intent. Let the rust, the rustling, the groaning, or the silence do the work.

Secondly, you are overusing the phrase 'the way.' It appears in ch01 ('the way it held color'), ch04 ('the way her fabric clung', 'the way she held her weight', 'the way she would hear a reef shifting'), and ch08 ('the way a surgeon reads a pulse'). This construction is filtering: you are describing a simile through a lens of observation rather than committing to the comparison. Cut the 'the way' and trust the metaphor. 'Her fabric clung and released' is stronger than 'the way her fabric clung and released.'

Finally, your punctuation is collapsing. You have a compulsion for the em-dash that turns every paragraph into a single, breathless clause. In ch08, you use 'He thought of...' as a rhythmic device, stacking eight sentences that all begin with the same word. This creates monotony, not intensity. Break the chains. Use periods. Let the reader breathe.

— Editor

### Mechanics & continuity (copy editor)

The Deep Remembered carries the weight of Melville and the rot of Conrad, but the machinery is leaking from the inside out. I have read the bible, and I know who these people are. The problem is that the manuscript seems to have forgotten its own cast list. In Chapter 3, the man we spend Chapter 2 watching—Anselm Broussard—is suddenly spelled "Brouards" for the remainder of that chapter. That is not a typo; that is a ghost. In Chapter 5, the woman who arrives with the glass shard—Ayaanle—is asked about her companion, and Nemo calls her "Anselm." He then points to a "glass shard wrapped in cloth" that is actually a piece of her sister's memory. In Chapter 7, the engagement happens, but the text claims "three men" are sharing the deep, when the crew is two men and one woman. In Chapter 10, the storm hits, and Broussard is left behind, but in Chapter 13, he is loading torpedoes. In Chapter 14, Nemo is in the engine room, but in Chapter 13, he is on the conning tower. The manuscript is drifting, and I have flagged the eight most critical breaches. Fix them, or the reader will be lost in the deep.

### Final pass (proofreader)

You have written a manuscript that breathes, but you have given it asthma in Chapter 3. The text in [ch03] suffers from a catastrophic mechanical failure—specifically, a 'save' button that was stuck. Paragraphs 61 through 74 are garbled with truncated lines, duplicate sentences, and fragments that read like the engine room of the *Aletheia* itself has developed a cough. "He had no idea what the stranger intended. That was also enough." appears five times in as many paragraphs. It is not enough. It is exhausting.

Beyond the corruption in Ch3, I have found three specific mechanical errors that will trip the typesetter. First, in Chapter 2, you refer to the dhow as "a Arab dhow." The article must be 'an.' Second, in Chapter 5, you have an orphaned horizontal rule (---) in paragraph 10 that does nothing; it breaks the flow between Nemo arming himself and Broussard arriving. Third, in Chapter 11, you address the captain as "Nero" before correcting to "Captain." While intentional, the typo "Nero" looks like a name error rather than a beat, because it is not italicized or flagged. Fix these, and the book stops bleeding.

The prose itself is dense, muscular, and committed. I am not touching the themes—the revenge arc, the use of Broussard as a tool, the literal breathing of the ship. Those are your choices. My job is to ensure the reader isn't tripping over your typos while they are admiring your metaphors. The Ch3 cleanup is non-negotiable; it currently reads as a drafting error, not a stylistic one.

## My recommended revision priorities

1. **Raise the stakes of the mutiny. If Ayaanle is right and Nemo is wrong, Nemo must lose control completely, not just 'find children.' Make the mutiny nearly catastrophic.** (ch07 ¶5)
   > trusting her memory of the currents over his memory of the battle
2. **Cut Chapter 14 entirely. Merge the escape into Chapter 15. Reduce the repetition of 'the first time the destination had changed' to zero. One passage, one outcome.** (ch15 ¶12)
   > She did not look back. She did not need to. She knew where she was going.
3. **Delete the second iteration of the sky description. It reads as a copy-paste error.** (ch01 ¶6)
   > Above, a sky the color of bruised indigo... [same text repeated immediately after]
4. **Change 'Anselm's companion' to 'Ayaanle's companion' or 'the navigator'.** (ch05 ¶4)
   > "You are Anselm's companion," Nemo said.
5. **Change 'three men' to 'three souls' or 'the crew'.** (ch07 ¶2)
   > Now he asked three men to share it.

—Chief Editor
