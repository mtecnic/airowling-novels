## Title
The Weight of Brass

## Premise
In the economic twilight of Steubenville, Ohio, a retired scrap-metal recycler discovers a ring belonging to his murdered sister-in-law among the debris of an estate sale, forcing him to uncover a truth that threatens his son’s career, his pastor brother-in-law’s soul, and the fragile peace of a dying mill town.

## Themes
Grief as labor, the cost of silence, community complicity

## Voice
Third-person limited, past tense, restrained and tactile, focusing on the physical weight of objects and the quiet erosion of time.

## Characters
**Walter "Wally" Bellamy**: protagonist — wants to know the truth about Janine’s death without destroying his son’s future — speaks in measured, complete sentences; listens more than he talks; wears his wedding ring on a chain — eyes: blue-gray — hair: thinning white — age: 61

**Danny Bellamy**: son — wants to become Sheriff to secure his family’s legacy and provide for his son — competent, cautious, avoids conflict; checks his watch when anxious — eyes: brown — hair: dark brown, short — age: 34
  Active Arc: Danny arrests a local drunk during a routine patrol in Chapter 5, refusing to cut him loose despite pleas, establishing his rigid adherence to protocol which later complicates his handling of the Janine case.

**Reverend Dean Miller**: brother-in-law — wants to maintain the stability of his church and marriage — polite, theological, avoids direct eye contact when lying; hums hymns when nervous — eyes: hazel — hair: gray, receding — age: 58
  Active Arc: Dean organizes a town-wide food drive in Chapter 9, using the event to subtly manipulate local donors into supporting the Sheriff’s campaign, demonstrating his active investment in the status quo.

**Ruth Hollister**: widow — wants to understand the strange artifact her husband brought home — sharp, dry wit; taps her cane rhythmically when thinking; plays cribbage aggressively — eyes: green — hair: white, pinned back — age: 84
  Active Arc: Ruth confronts Wally in Chapter 10, demanding he stop visiting her if he isn’t going to solve the mystery, forcing Wally to commit to the investigation rather than treating it as a hobby.

**John Hollister**: stepson of Ruth, the killer — wants to preserve his reputation as a pillar of the community — charming, deferential, uses religious language to mask guilt; never raises his voice — eyes: brown — hair: blond, greying — age: 45
  Active Arc: John leads a prayer group at the church in Chapter 12, actively recruiting new members to bolster the church’s image, unaware that his presence is being scrutinized by Wally.

**Salvador**: coonhound — wants comfort and safety — limps heavily; sleeps near the radiator; whines softly at night — eyes: amber — hair: black and tan — age: 12

## Settings
**Bellamy Salvage Yard**: A sprawling lot of rusted metal and silence, smelling of ozone and wet iron, where the Ohio River fog settles thick against the chain-link fence.

**First Presbyterian Church Parsonage**: A drafty Victorian house with peeling paint, smelling of stale coffee and lemon polish, where shadows stretch long across the hardwood floors.

**Ruth Hollister’s Apartment**: A cramped second-floor flat in Weirton, cluttered with card tables and empty pill bottles, smelling of mothballs and burnt toast.

**The Ohio River Bank**: A muddy, sludge-stained edge of water, smelling of diesel and decay, where barges move slowly like sleeping beasts.

## Plot Points
1. (setup, ch03) Wally finds the dented brass jewelry box in the crusher feed; he pulls it out just before the blades engage, recognizing the initials "JB → EL".
2. (foreshadow, ch05) Danny arrests a local man for DUI, refusing to let him go despite the man’s connection to the Sheriff’s donor list, showing Danny’s rigid integrity.
3. (midpoint, ch08) Wally meets Ruth Hollister; she reveals her husband bought the box in 1988 from a man who claimed it was "junk," but Ruth suspects otherwise.
4. (reversal, ch10) Wally realizes the "boyfriend" Janine mentioned was John Hollister, Ruth’s stepson, not a stranger; the killer is within the community, not a transient trucker.
5. (payoff, ch12) Salvador dies in the yard office; Wally breaks down, realizing his investigation was a way to avoid grieving Eileen, and now he must face both losses.
6. (climax, ch14) Wally confronts Dean in the parsonage; Dean admits he knew John killed Janine but stayed silent to protect his marriage and the church’s stability.

## Chapters
1. Wally sorts scrap at dawn, the silence of the yard pressing on him; he notices Salvador’s limp worsening.
2. Danny visits the yard, bringing coffee; they talk about the upcoming Sheriff election, the tension between duty and desire palpable.
3. The brass box arrives in the crusher; Wally stops the machine, pulling out the ring with Janine’s initials, his breath catching in his throat.
4. Wally researches the box’s origin, driving to Weirton; the mill closure signs loom large, a backdrop of economic despair.
5. Danny handles a domestic dispute, his professionalism masking his fear of failing his father’s expectations; he returns to the yard, uneasy.
6. Wally visits Ruth Hollister’s apartment; she invites him to play cribbage, her sharp eyes missing nothing.
7. Wally and Ruth play cards; she shares anecdotes about her husband, the silence between them heavy with unspoken questions.
8. Ruth reveals her husband’s purchase of the box; Wally connects the dots to John Hollister, the realization hitting him with physical force.
9. Dean hosts a church dinner; Wally attends, observing Dean’s polished demeanor, sensing the cracks beneath.
10. Ruth confronts Wally, demanding answers; he commits to finding the truth, driven by a mix of grief and duty.
11. Wally investigates John Hollister, watching him from afar; the moral weight of the potential arrest presses on him.
12. Salvador dies in the yard office; Wally holds the dog, finally allowing himself to grieve for Eileen and the lost years.
13. Danny learns of Wally’s investigation; the strain between them peaks as Danny realizes the truth could ruin his career.
14. Wally confronts Dean in the parsonage; Dean confesses his knowledge, the conversation quiet and devastating.
15. Wally gives the ring to Danny; the mill closes, Salvador is buried, and Wally accepts the end, the river flowing on.

## Pacing
Tension builds slowly through Wally’s quiet investigations and the deteriorating health of Salvador. The midpoint reversal occurs in Chapter 8 when the scope of the conspiracy expands from a random act to a familial betrayal. The climax lands in Chapter 14 with the confrontation with Dean, followed by the emotional resolution in Chapter 15 where Wally accepts the irreversible losses.

## Irrevocable Loss
Wally loses his dog Salvador, his last companion, and his hope for a clean resolution to Janine’s murder. He also loses his health, diagnosed with emphysema, and his son’s trust, as Danny must choose between justice and his career. The mill closes, taking the town’s identity with it. Wally does not live to see Danny win the Sheriff’s race, leaving him with the knowledge that the truth came at the cost of his remaining time.
