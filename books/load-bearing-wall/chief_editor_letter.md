# Editor's Letter — The Load-Bearing Wall

Dear author,

Four editors read your manuscript. Each pass surfaces something different. Their voices follow.

## Where the book stands

### Story (developmental editor)

Your premise is strong, and the voice is consistently engaging, but the narrative architecture is fundamentally broken by a structural contradiction: you have written 15 chapters in your outline but provided a manuscript for only 14. More critically, the climax in Chapter 13 feels unearned because the antagonist—the external pressure of corporate/structural failure—is resolved too neatly by a single improvised fix. The inspector scene lacks genuine risk; if Steve can just 'improvise' his way past a code violation, the stakes evaporate. You need to raise the cost of this victory.

Furthermore, the emotional arc of Steve’s relationship with Owen is stunted. In Chapter 5, Steve declares, 'We’re doing this ourselves,' yet by Chapter 11, Owen is teaching Steve drywalling. This reverses the power dynamic without showing the painful negotiation that should precede it. The 'viral video' hook in Chapter 2 is brilliant, but it disappears from the plot after Chapter 4. It needs to haunt the resolution, not just fade away. Finally, Doug’s arc is the strongest thread, but he is sidelined in the final act. He knows the building’s secrets, yet he doesn’t drive the final solution. He should be the one who saves the store, not Steve improvising.

### Mechanics & continuity (copy editor)

Dear Author,

The emotional core of this manuscript is strong, but the mechanics are currently undermining the credibility of the setting and the characters. The most glaring issue is the timeline: you establish the collapse happens on a Tuesday, yet in Chapter 3, Steve reflects that he has been sitting in his truck for "three days" and Mei-Lin states she has been sorting since "six" that morning, implying the collapse happened on a Saturday. This breaks the continuity established in Ch1 and Ch2. Furthermore, there is a persistent physical inconsistency with Owen’s footwear. In Ch3, he tries on Steve’s steel-toed boots, but by Ch4 and Ch5, he is back in his "scuffed sneakers" without any explanation of how or why he switched back, especially given the hazardous environment. Finally, Doug’s backstory has drifted; he claims to have drawn the original blueprints in Ch7, but the Bible explicitly states he was hired in 1981, while the building was constructed in 1978. Unless he was an intern or junior architect before joining staff, this contradicts the canon. Please tighten these details to ensure the reader’s immersion isn’t broken by avoidable errors.

Best,
Your Copy Editor

### Final pass (proofreader)

I have completed the final mechanical pass on this manuscript. The prose is generally strong, with a distinct voice that balances technical detail with emotional resonance. However, there are a few glaring mechanical errors that must be addressed before print, ranging from missing quotation marks to accidental repetition of text. These are not stylistic choices; they are artifacts that will distract the reader and undermine the authority of the narrative. Specifically, the dialogue in Chapter 2 contains a significant formatting break, and Chapter 11 ends with a sentence fragment that suggests a file corruption or copy-paste error. Please review the findings below carefully.

## My recommended revision priorities

1. **Replace the improvisation with a moment where Steve must admit ignorance and defer to Doug’s knowledge of the building’s original, non-compliant structure, forcing Steve to trust his son and Doug over his own corporate training.** (ch13 ¶3)
   > Steve improvises a solution using scavenged parts, risking his license to keep moving.
2. **Restructure Ch13 so that the inspector’s rejection is resolved specifically by Doug’s hand-drawn plans, validating Doug’s expertise and Steve’s acceptance of it.** (ch09 ¶1)
   > Doug reveals the original building plans were hand-drawn and hidden in the store’s old filing cabinet.
3. **Clarify the day of the week in Ch3. If the collapse was Tuesday, Mei-Lin's 'since six' should refer to Monday morning or the weekend, and Steve's 'three days' must align with the current day (e.g., Friday).** (ch03 ¶42)
   > "It was a rhythmic thumping... Since six," she said. "I figured if we’re going to rebuild, we need to know what we have.…
4. **Add a brief line in Ch4 or Ch5 explaining why Owen is back in his sneakers (e.g., they didn't fit, he preferred them, or Steve insisted he change for safety reasons).** (ch04 ¶15)
   > "He was wearing the same clothes he'd worn yesterday—jeans, a hoodie, sneakers that had seen better days."
5. **Adjust Doug's dialogue to reflect that he assisted the architect or was an apprentice, OR change the Bible entry to state Doug was hired in 1978 as a junior architect. Given the Bible says he was hired in 1981, the latter is a major plot shift; better to change Doug's line to: 'I was the junior architect's assistant,' or 'I drafted the revisions,' to fit the 1981 hire date.** (ch07 ¶18)
   > "I drew them," Doug said. "Junior architect,' Doug said. 'Before I realized I liked selling things better than designing…
